Proposed model · version 0.1

Where is your website, from 0 to 5?

Sovereignty is not yes or no; it is how far you can move, on your terms. This model places a website on six levels, from rented to sovereign and proven.

What is a sovereign website

How to read it

Start at level 0 and read up. A site sits at the highest level whose requirements it meets in full. The seven principles say what a sovereign website is; the levels show how far along the way a site has come.

The six levels

  1. Level 0 Rented

    A hosted builder decides everything, and little leaves but some content. Most website platforms sit here by their own terms of service.

    Requires: nothing of the owner. The platform holds the site, and its terms decide what you may take.

    To move up: be able to export your words and images.

  2. Level 1 Exportable

    You can export your words and images; the design and the functions stay behind.

    Requires: an export of your content that you can take away.

    To move up: run the site on open software you can host yourself.

  3. Level 2 Self-hosted, with a part you do not control

    The software is open, but one paid plugin, theme or agency component holds the site where it is.

    Requires: open software, self-hosted. One proprietary part is still in the middle, and that one part is enough to stop a move (principle 3).

    To move up: replace that part, so that the code in your repository is open throughout.

  4. Level 3 Open, on one host

    Open code in your repository, but tied to one host's special features.

    Requires: open code, in a repository you own.

    To move up: stop depending on the host's special features, so any host that serves files can serve the site.

  5. Level 4 Portable

    Plain files and open tools; any static host can serve it; your domain at your registrar. The website cell is here today.

    Requires: plain files and open tools, servable by any static host without changes (principle 2), and the domain registered to you, at a registrar you chose.

    To move up: prove it, by deploying the same site on more than one host, regularly.

  6. Level 5 Sovereign and proven

    Portable, and proven by being deployed on many hosts, one of them in Europe and one your own machine.

    Requires: everything at level 4, and deployment on many hosts, including one in Europe and one on your own machine (principle 7).

    Then: keep it proven. Moving is a thing that already happened, not a promise.

The same model inside a company

The model works for a corporate site too. A corporate site built on one cloud provider's proprietary services sits at level 3 at best, however well it is engineered: it cannot move without rebuilding. That is the question many European organisations are now asking of their cloud providers, and it is the same question, smaller, for every website.

Level 5 is not reached yet

Level 5 needs a pipeline that deploys the same commit to many hosts. That pipeline does not exist yet; our sites deploy to GitHub Pages only today. When it exists, the proposal is that every Sovereign Agentic Website carries that proof. See the proof.

A self-check, later

A self-assessment that gives a site its level is planned, as a next step. Until then, the levels and the tests on the principles page are the self-check: read them against your own site.

What this does not claim

  • The model is a proposal, version 0.1, written to be argued with and improved.
  • Platform placements on the model are by their published terms, as in no lock-in; a given site may do better or worse.
  • The "requires" and "to move up" lines are a plain reading of each level, open for comment.
  • The every-host pipeline does not exist yet; only GitHub Pages is in use today.
